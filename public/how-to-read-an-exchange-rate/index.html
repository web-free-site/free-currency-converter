How to read an exchange rate

An exchange rate tells you how much of one currency you get for a unit of another. The number you see on a free website is usually an indicative mid-market style rate. The rate your bank, bureau, or mobile-money operator uses to sell or buy currency will often be different because of their spread and fees.

Steps

  1. Identify the pair. A pair such as USD/RWF or RWF to USD states which currency you start from and which you convert into. Reversing the pair gives a different display, but it describes the same market relationship.
  2. Enter an amount in the converter. Open Free Currency Converter, choose the two currencies, and enter an amount. The tool uses a public rate feed (open.er-api.com, with a fallback if needed).
  3. Treat the result as a reference, not a quote. The converted amount is a guide. Before you send money, compare it with the rate and fees shown by your provider.
  4. Note the date and source. Rates move. If you need a record, write down the time and that the figure was indicative.

Why cash and transfer rates differ

A bureau selling banknotes, a card used abroad, and a bank transfer can all apply different margins to the same underlying market rate. Fees may be separate from the rate itself.
